# Round 1 — reviewer 04: newcomer from TikTok

## Perspective and limits

I am simulating a woman who heard about Strata on TikTok and wants to try her first conversation. Her prior experience with local AI is unknown; no technical knowledge is assumed. Gender supplies no assumptions about ability, taste or behavior. The relevant motivation is curiosity and a quick first successful chat, with an understandable download commitment and a safe way back when something fails.

This is a persona-based design critique, not observed usability research. Statements about confusion, hesitation or abandonment are hypotheses to test with actual newcomers. I reviewed independently, without reading other persona reports. No browser, server, engine or model was run.

## Evidence and assessment

Reviewed `docs/engine-models-onboarding-2026-10-05.md`, interaction guidelines (especially one hero per screen, readable prose, keyboard/focus handling, interruptible actions and reduced motion), and current `Tuning.tsx`, `Models.tsx`, `Setup.tsx`, `EngineExperience.tsx`, `ModelCatalog.tsx` plus the profile constants in `experience.ts`.

Inspected screenshots `01-engine-light.png`, `07-models-light.png`, `09-onboarding-profile.png`, `08-variant-comparison.png`, `10-onboarding-mobile.png` in `docs/evidence/instrument-redesign-2026-10-05/`. Current source takes precedence when screenshots differ: setup defaults to Balanced, although the captured selection is Performance; source includes the Response settings disclosure. Steps 1, 3, 4 and 5 were assessed from source, not interactive observation.

The design already has valuable safeguards: optional setup tuning; a default Balanced profile; explicit simulation labels; saved progress; cancellation; exact model variants; a distinct load action; and loading required before Chat. Keep these. The friction is the amount of simultaneous explanation and technical data. Engine presents two unfamiliar controls, exact inputs, instructions, memory segments and both speed metrics at once. Models repeats the same exact variant and statistics across Balanced and Performance. Setup repeats the selected profile purpose inside the recommendation. On the 390px screenshot, the recommendation begins near the bottom and its action is out of view. Comparison repeats whole performance cards instead of presenting aligned decision facts.

## Three must-do choices

1. **N04 — Shorten model cards around exact choice, fit, download and action.** This directly addresses the complaint and establishes a reusable card for onboarding.
2. **N09 — Simplify setup step 2 and remove duplicated purpose/selection controls.** The mobile screenshot is concrete evidence of the vertical cost; the predicted reduction in hesitation still needs research.
3. **N10 — Make the download commitment visible before it starts, with recommended settings as the easy path.** First chat depends on downloading/loading successfully, so the action should describe that commitment.

N01/N02 and N11 are also high priority, but these three give the smallest coherent improvement to the first-use path.

## Two proposals I oppose

1. **Oppose replacing evidence with friendly invented labels such as “Instant answers” or “Fast on your computer”, and hiding Demo badges for visual simplicity.** Screenshots show illustrative ranges, not a machine benchmark. Friendly copy cannot turn simulation into measured performance. Keep unknown first-answer delay unknown; no fabricated download ETA.
2. **Oppose deleting Expert/Raw, exact variants or comparison to make the app “beginner friendly”.** A first-time user may grow into those controls or arrive with a specific recommendation. Progressive disclosure reduces the initial burden while preserving access, recovery and transparency.

## Validation to request later

Ask actual first-time visitors to choose a starter model, explain what will download and what “Demo” means, reach a first chat, then recover from a cancelled download and a failed load. Compare current and shortened cards for task success and comprehension; do not use this simulated review as proof of a measured usability gain. Native hookup remains deferred.
