# Reviewer 08 — workplace user, round 2

Read all eight original reports and ballot.md. These are simulated design judgments, not findings from eight human participants. I remain a frequent workplace AI user interested in document revision, translation, spreadsheets, chat and contracts, with predictable setup and little jargon. No app edits, browser/server/model execution or delegation occurred. Native integration and production task evidence remain deferred.

## Challenges to peers

1. **02-mac M05: one selected recommendation hides too much of the available choice.** I oppose SELECTOR in B06 and vote UNIQUE. One selected card is compact, but it makes me visit each priority to discover that two names produce one exact model or that no reviewed tool candidate fits. One card per unique winner plus profile chips and a compact no-fit row removes duplication while keeping those decision facts visible. I accept M05's removal of repeated slogans and purposes; I challenge its selector-only presentation, not its simplification goal.

2. **05-tinkerer proposal 5: moving approval/review status entirely into Why this choice is insufficient for ordinary recommendations.** I vote VISIBLE in B19. Provisional describes confidence without telling me whether the missing evidence concerns speed or capabilities. A single actual review-status line can replace the generic tradeoff paragraph already occupying space. I do not want another paragraph or a task checklist. The line should be scoped to the real metadata, such as Tool review pending, and cannot say spreadsheet-ready or safe for contracts. Detailed evaluation, rationale and absent task evidence belong in Why this model.

3. **03-teacher T06: “Show other file formats (quantizations)” is misleading vocabulary.** Quantization is not an XLSX/PDF-style file-support choice. Keep exact quantization readable, but use Show file variants or Show quantizations with a short definition in help. My round-one file-variant proposal already required that distinction; I hold it after this wording comparison.

## Concession and changed positions

**04-newcomer N02 persuades me to move Engine's rates and sample controls into Speed details.** I originally kept modest reading/answer rates visible (#4). N02's argument that default feedback should answer whether the setup fits applies to repeated workplace use as well as first use. The numbers in the current captures are illustrative, and my ordinary capacity change does not require comparing throughput. I therefore vote MEMORY for B03 and FIT for B08. Comparison still presents both rates because comparing differences is its explicit task. This is a genuine change, not agreement manufactured for the ballot.

**06-parallel proposals 1 and 4 add a useful consequence I underweighted.** Even my ordinary document chats share an engine. Concise global scope and known active/queued blockers belong beside lifecycle actions, with unknown state kept honest. I support B17 without adding idle counts or promising parallel inference.

**03-teacher T04 also exposes an error in my own #1 wording.** “Changes take effect after saving” overgeneralizes next-request and next-load behavior. I withdraw that phrase. The amendment states draft/save state accurately and preserves effect timing beside edited response controls and explicit restart review.

## Held positions

I retain a separately named five-step flow (B12 FIVE). The third step can be Review setup with adjustment collapsed; it gives the exact model, estimated fit and actual download amount a predictable commitment point. B14 shortens that review without deleting it. I retain plain visible profile names (B13 RENAME), exact variant identity (B07), shared winners (B06 UNIQUE), aligned comparison (B11) and the loaded readiness gate (B16).

**B19 remains VISIBLE despite the extra-text objection.** The ballot replaces generic tradeoff prose rather than appending more teaching. A short review status is more informative than “A practical starting point for everyday work.” It must faithfully scope actual metadata; absent workplace evaluations cannot become a universal unreviewed verdict or a positive office guarantee. I hold the information hierarchy principle from #5 while narrowing the wording to what has actually been reviewed. This is a held position with a scope refinement.

## Priorities and limits

Top three: **B19** distinguishes recommendation from reviewed task capability; **B11** removes repeated comparison panels; **B14** keeps a compact, explicit download/configuration review. All twenty exact votes and non-voting amendments are in 08-office-vote.json. No vote is conditional. These recommendations need real user testing; none establishes XLSX manipulation, translation quality, tool safety, legal reliability, confidentiality or measured native performance.
