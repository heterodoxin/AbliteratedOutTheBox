# Persona review 03 — teacher learning local models

## Perspective and goals

This is a simulated perspective of an older teacher who handles ordinary PC tasks comfortably and is learning local LLMs. It is not a report of an observed participant. I would want to prepare lessons and discuss documents, know which choices consume computer resources, and recover from an experiment without losing a working setup. Familiar language and readable text matter more than hiding all technical terms. Age does not imply inability to learn the controls.

My recommendation is to reduce mandatory reading by removing repeated explanations and disclosing technical mechanisms, while keeping the immediate consequence and recovery action beside each consequential control.

## Three must-do changes

1. **T04 — make saving, restarting and recovery unambiguous.** Distinct draft/saved/applied states and explicit destinations for revert/reset are the foundation for exploring safely.
2. **T10 — review download and memory consequences before preparation.** File size, fit and exact selected variant belong at the commitment point.
3. **T11 — show cancellation/retry consequences beside the action.** Restarting an unfinished download is essential information before cancelling, not optional technical background.

## Two proposals to oppose

1. **Make the interface fit by shrinking text or removing labels from controls.** This reduces legibility and teaches no useful meaning; instead remove repeated sentences and disclose mechanisms.
2. **Hide all warnings, evidence status and consequences in a universal Details drawer.** Demo status, unknown evidence, over-budget state, effect timing and cancellation consequences must remain where the user decides. They are short decision information, not optional documentation.

## Independent round 1 conclusion

The screens already offer substantial recovery and expert access. The strongest improvements reduce duplicate reading and move secondary mechanisms behind clearly named disclosures. Retain enough immediate meaning that a new local-model user can predict what saving, downloading, loading and cancelling will do. Any later ballot should treat these as design judgements from a simulated perspective, not empirical user findings.
