# Persona 01 — PC gamer

## Goals and evidence

I want a quick, attractive settings interface that tells me what changing a value buys me and what it costs in memory. I am comfortable with hardware, performance settings and exact technical names, but I do not want to read introductory paragraphs every time I tune. This is a simulated persona perspective, not user research.

Inspected the implementation report `docs/engine-models-onboarding-2026-10-05.md`; design-system notes; interaction guidelines, particularly focused views, density, interruptibility, reduced motion and content surfaces; and actual `Tuning.tsx`, `Models.tsx`, `Setup.tsx`, `EngineExperience.tsx` and `ModelCatalog.tsx`. Visually inspected saved screenshots 01 Engine, 07 Models, 08 comparison, 09 onboarding profile, plus 02 Computer, 03 Ready and 10 mobile onboarding. Current source/screenshots take precedence over historical design-system claims. No app launched, source changed or model run.

## First impression

**Evidence:** The dials and large speed numbers make Engine attractive and scannable. The same screen also displays a promotional heading, draft reassurance, two interaction instructions, two long dial explanations, three duration stages, memory explanation and a long impact sentence. Models puts the same exact variant and repeated metrics in two large recommendation cards. Mobile onboarding visibly repeats Performance's purpose immediately after the selected profile tile; the recommendation begins near the bottom of the captured viewport.

**Persona hypothesis:** I would trust the interface more when concise facts dominate. The heavy explanatory layer makes a relatively simple adjustment feel like a tutorial. I would still open details before committing a model change or interpreting a benchmark. These reactions are hypotheses; no completion-time, preference or comprehension test was performed.

## Three must-do changes

1. **G05:** Reduce recommendation cards to exact identity, metrics, fit, concise uncertainty and action; use the existing rationale disclosure.
2. **G01 + G02 as one Engine pass:** Remove the marketing header and repeated dial prose while retaining precise inputs and a clear draft state.
3. **G09:** Eliminate repeated profile explanation in the selected onboarding recommendation, especially on mobile.

## Two changes I oppose

1. Hiding exact quantization names or collapsing all variants into an opaque “best model” choice. I want to compare files and retain direct Expert/Raw access; concise UI should not reduce the available controls.
2. Removing Demo, unsupported, provisional, fit or capability warnings to make cards prettier, or adding a single performance/quality score. Numeric speed ranges already look convincing; their evidence status needs visible labels. Native hookup remains deferred.

## Round-one recommendation

Use progressive disclosure to remove repetition, with numbers and controls remaining prominent. Keep fit problems and evidence status visible. My preference for quick scanning is a persona hypothesis; the duplicated text and tall cards are directly observable evidence.
